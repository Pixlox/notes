A British company called Vestys had brought the land from the government years before, and they were (surprisingly) prepared to return the land back to these people. However, there was strong opposition to this possibility, which prevented the Gurindji people from claiming the land back.

They continued to protest, even sending a petition to the Governor General in 1967 and going on tour around Australia trying to raise awareness. But it wasn't until 1972 when Prime mInister Gough Whitlam finally put plans into action. He provided money on behalf of the Gurindji people for purchasing the traditional lands that were not considered reserves. Vestys additionally surrendered 90% of their lands to the traditional owners too.

This was a 'landmark decision' (meaning the first decision of its kind) which would begin the ongoing fight for the return of traditional lands to Aboriginal people.

1. Indigenous Australians suffered a lack of rights in the majority of states across Australia. Across WA, NT and QLD, Indigenous Australians had no right of free movement, ownership of property, free [[Wages]], and in WA and QLD, no voting rights. States such as VIC and NSW were more advanced, allowing Indigenous people to vote, marry freely, and in NSW, receive award [[Wages]] and move freely; something no other state did.
